Aging is a major risk factor for many diseases, yet the cellular mechanisms linking aging to disease development remain poorly understood. While most studies focus on disease-specific risk factors, the contribution of age-associated cellular changes is often overlooked. Through whole-organism single-cell transcriptomics and cross-species comparisons, we have identified distinct transcriptional responses to aging and disease, giving rise to heterogeneous cellular states. We hypothesize that aging- and disease-associated cell populations may interact to promote cellular dysfunction and accelerate disease progression. Our goal is to uncover these interactions and understand how cellular aging contributes to the development of age-related diseases.
